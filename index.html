FilterPhone · Mock build

One request.
Every supplier bids.

FilterPhone is a real-time broker between the people who need filters and the suppliers who sell them — both sides in a single build, run as an open market, the way the freight market works. The on-site customer posts a request for quote (an RFQ). Suppliers see the RFQs that fit their own constraints — catalog, stock, coverage, windows — and choose to bid or pass. The customer sees only bids: the vendor's name, the total, and the delivery window that vendor is promising. Everyone else is silence, and that's correct — a customer doesn't know, or care, why someone didn't bid.

  1. The customer talks. Hands busy, so he speaks the RFQ: “Two cases of 20 by 25 by 1, MERV 8 — and one case of 16 by 20 by 4, MERV 13.” It transcribes and parses into lines.
  2. Post it to the market. A saved job-site card and the delivery window he requires, then the RFQ goes out open.
  3. Vendors bid — or don't. Switch to the vendor side (top of the phone), pick a supplier, and bid on the live RFQ from your own price book, promising a delivery window inside the customer's range — or pass, silently. No reason is sent; the customer never knows you saw it. The other vendors' bids are simulated from their price books.
  4. Pick on total and window. Bid cards carry three things: name, total, promised window. Cost has the most influence, but a tighter window can win the order. Tap a bid for its details — the charge breakdown and optional services live there, not on the card — then lock the winner by text code.

Demo data only. Every person, company, site, price, and stock count on this page is invented, vendor bids are simulated from that data except any bid you place yourself on the vendor side, and no order goes anywhere. Where the browser supports it, the mic uses real speech recognition and the read-back uses real speech synthesis; otherwise the mock's demo voice stands in so the flow can still be walked end to end.
